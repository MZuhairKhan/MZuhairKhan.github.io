**Designing a 3D Cavity for Photon-Number Cooling Using a Quantum Circuit Refrigerator**

This Bachelor's thesis was completed at the **Pico Group**, Aalto University, supervised by Matti Raasakka and advised by Shuji Nakamura, as part of the B.Sc in Quantum Technology (graduated with Honours, June 2024).

The project set out to design and characterise **3D cavity resonators** coupled to a **quantum circuit refrigerator (QCR)** — a device that can actively cool the photon population of a mode — working toward photon-number cooling. I worked with aluminium and copper cavities, taking transmission and reflection spectra on a Vector Network Analyser at room temperature and then at millikelvin temperatures in a cryogen-free dilution refrigerator, and ran finite-element (FEM) simulations on Aalto's Triton cluster to compare against.

The measured cavities reached quality factors of roughly **67,000** (aluminium) and **12,000** (copper), well short of the simulated values, and the thesis works through where that shortfall comes from.
